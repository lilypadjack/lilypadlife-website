

LILY PAD LIFE — YOUR NEXT LILY PAD IS CALLING An audio introduction adapted from the manuscript by Jack Cardoza 



There has to be more to my life than this. Perhaps you've heard that quiet voice. 

It may arrive after a promotion, a graduation, a retirement, or simply on an ordinary morning when the life you've built no longer feels like the whole story. I call it the Something More Calling.

I'm Jack Cardoza, and I've come to see life as a series of lily pads. Each one gives us a place to learn, grow, and discover capacities we didn't know we possessed. 

Then, one day, something new begins calling. What got you here may not get you there.

My own life has taken some unlikely turns. In Vietnam I wanted to fly helicopters. When I lost flight status, I was furious. Later, an Army sergeant invited me to help restore our grounded helicopter unit. I brought together a team of resourceful misfits, and we helped get aircraft flying again. I thought my story was about flying. It turned out to be about leadership. 

Years later, on a meditation retreat high in California's Sierra Nevada mountains sparked a wilderness guiding business. Around campfires I discovered that people weren't just learning about the outdoors. They were discovering personal aspects, like courage in themselves. 

Then there was Titanic. The newspaper article said the movie had filled all 1500 of its extra positions. I drove to the set anyway, asked a guard a question, and wound up working on the film for five and a half months. That experience opened a completely unexpected business in movie props. 

These weren't steps in some master plan. They began with curiosity, hunches, and the willingness to go look. The philosopher Buckminster Fuller gave me a word for some of this: precession. We aim at one thing, and an unexpected contribution emerges along the way. The visible goal may be only the doorway. But curiosity needs a partner: the practical world. I listen to two conversations. The outer one asks: what does the world need, and where can I contribute? The inner one asks: what comes alive in me, and who am I becoming? A meaningful next lily pad can emerge where those conversations meet. 

You don't always need a dramatic leap. Sometimes you need a bridge lily pad: work that supports your life and gives your next chapter time to develop. I've driven early morning airport rides to create exactly that breathing room. 

Usually, the most important transformation is inward. Your internal GPS may offer only the next breadcrumb, not a complete route. Self-love helps you trust yourself enough to follow it. Self-love produces self-permission. Permission leads to experimentation. Experimentation produces information. Information brings clarity. 

Ask yourself: What keeps returning to my attention? What gives me energy? What am I resisting? What part of me is ready to emerge? You don't have to know the whole path. Begin somewhere small and real. Go look. Ask the question. See what's there. A lily pad isn't a final destination. It is both a place to stand and a place from which to leap. Life isn't asking you for perfection. Life is asking you for participation.
